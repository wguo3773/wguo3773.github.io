Bio

I am an oncology statistician at the University of Maryland Greenebaum Comprehensive Cancer Center. I develop statistical and machine learning methods for high-dimensional biomedical data and collaborate on translational and clinical oncology research. My work spans robust variable selection, causal machine learning, multi-omics transfer learning, real-world evidence, and survival analysis.

I earned an M.S. in Biostatistics from the University of Florida, where I received the Outstanding Achievement Award. Previously, I completed a fully funded thesis-based M.S. at McGill University as a Graduate Excellence Fellow and graduated with First Class Honours from the University of Queensland in Australia, where I received Dean's Commendations and a Chancellor's Scholarship.

High-dimensional inference Causal machine learning Clinical oncology Real-world evidence Multi-omics

Highlights

  • Developed two-stage Virtual Twins causal machine learning models to identify genomic subgroups associated with chemotherapy benefit in ER+/HER2- invasive ductal breast cancer (Clinical Cancer Research; Cancer Genetics, 2025) and the first Virtual Twins framework to identify disparities in medication for opioid use disorder treatment discontinuation using nationwide longitudinal claims data (paper; thesis).
  • Developed a robust, scalable feature-selection method for ultra-high-dimensional correlated data using group testing, outperforming many powerful existing methods (Guo et al., 2026, arXiv; AACR 2026).
  • Developed a source-anchored, multi-omics transfer-learning method to improve prediction in young breast cancer and Black AML populations. Guo, Xie & Tatsuoka, 2026
  • Developed the first reported predictive model of tarlatamab toxicity in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (JCO/ASCO 2026; media coverage).
  • Conducted real-world evidence studies of AML outcomes by targetable mutation status (Blood, 2026) and smoking-related emergency department utilization using longitudinal causal inference (Nicotine & Tobacco Research, 2026+).
