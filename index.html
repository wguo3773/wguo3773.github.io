Bio

I am an oncology statistician at the University of Maryland Greenebaum Comprehensive Cancer Center. I develop statistical and machine learning methods for high-dimensional biomedical data and collaborate on translational and clinical oncology research.

My work spans robust variable selection, causal machine learning, multi-omics transfer learning, real-world evidence, and survival analysis. I am especially interested in methods that identify clinically meaningful subgroups and make complex health data more useful for treatment decisions.

High-dimensional inference Causal machine learning Clinical oncology Real-world evidence Multi-omics

Highlights

  • Developed a two-stage Virtual Twins causal machine learning model to identify genomic subgroups that may benefit from chemotherapy in ER+/HER2- invasive ductal breast cancer. Clinical Cancer Research · Cancer Genetics
  • Developed a robust, scalable feature-selection method for ultra-high-dimensional correlated data using group testing, outperforming CV-RFE, group lasso, sparse group lasso, adaptive elastic net/LASSO, Group-SIS, and fused LASSO. arXiv
  • Developed a source-anchored, weighted multi-omics elastic-net transfer learning method with tau-weighted borrowing across source and target populations to improve prediction for young patients with breast cancer and Black patients with acute myeloid leukemia. In preparation, 2026
  • Developed the first reported tumor-burden and biomarker predictors of response and toxicity to tarlatamab in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer. ASCO 2026 · Media coverage
  • Conducted the first longitudinal, nationwide real-world evidence study of smoking patterns, smoking cessation, and emergency department utilization using generalized estimating equations, propensity score methods, and time-dependent marginal structural models with inverse probability weighting. Submitted to Nicotine & Tobacco Research
  • Developed the first Virtual Twins machine learning framework for subgroup identification of disparities in medication for opioid use disorder treatment discontinuation using nationwide longitudinal claims data. Thesis, 2025
